Prototyping

Identify relevant artifacts from the human-centered design process that address the following points:

What design questions did your intend to answer with your prototype(s).

With our prototypes, we wanted to know what information should be shown on the main page for the planner and how should the user be able to interaact with it. We were asking ourselves whether it would be more useful to show progress information, like the number of credits and major requirements completed on the same page as the semester tables. We also thought of if we should keep it simple and only show the semesters and the courses, or maybe include stuff like saved plans, alternative plans and requirement tracking on the same page.

We also wanted to think about different users while we prototyped. So, we know that some students already know their major and just want to finish their schedule, while some others might be trying alternatives and might want to know more than one way. So another question we answered was how much stuff we put on the main page while not making it too crowded.

How did you design your prototype(s) and subsequent user studies to address these questions?

We planned our website through two different prototypes. One of them was one drawn on a whiteboard in class, where we focused more on what should be included like major selection, non-major interests, semester boxes, total credits and the major requirement progress. This first version was useful for us because it was a good base to have without discussing too much details.

Next, I spent some time in ChatGPT describing what our ideal page would look like including all the features like the semester grid, progress bars, alerts, credits and so forth. Using both of the prototypes helped us compare these base ideas into something more polished and we kind of had some thoughts as to which features where more useful and worth keeping and which ones would be overkill.

What answers to your design questions did your efforts unveil?

Our prototyping showed that students would probably benefit from having the schedule and progress information in the same place instead of separating them too much. The whiteboard version already suggested that requirement progress and credits taken were important to show, and the AI prototype made it clearer that these could fit be a good fit alongside the semester plan. This supported the idea that the planner should not just be a place to type courses, but also a place to understand whether you are on track or not to graduate. While we did not follow the template of AI 1:1, we did get some ideas from it. We did not want to overload the interface too much and some of the extra ideas we had like multiple schedules, we could not implement them in time. All in all, one big takeaway for us was that we should prioritize showing the course schedule first and then other features would follow from it.
