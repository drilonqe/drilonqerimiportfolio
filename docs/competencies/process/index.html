Process

What software engineering practices did your group employ to manage, distribute, and execute on their work?

As a group used a version of Agile Scrum to work on the project. We tried to follow it in some way and get some ideas from it. We divided the project into smaller weekly tasks, discussed about who was working on what, and worked in sprints where we focused on getting certain parts of the project working at a time. This worked well with how professor PM conducted the class which was also sprints, building an MVP and then working on it one step at a time.

Below is some of the notes we took for sprints:

Sprint 1.2 Restrospective and Planning

  • We underestimated the time it would take to learn a new coding language or tool, and we thought it would not be as difficult as it has been. Progress has been a little slower.
  • We prioritized learning and understanding in this first sprint, and now we will prioritize implementation and getting a product that we can showcase.
  • We will probably be more proactive and pick up the pace when it comes to development.

Sprint 1.3 Restrospective and Planning

  • In the previous sprint, we finally did some work that was not only abstract or conceptual, but something functional. It was helpful because, while doing the functional work, we learned a lot in our individual disciplines (React + Vite, JSON files, JavaScript, and so on).
  • We want to think about how we will move from small-scale (one major) to large-scale without having to hard-code the major requirements.
  • We want to be more efficient in how we separate our tasks and develop more quickly

In your estimation, how well did your group follow through on using these processes throughout the semester?

I think our group did well and followed through with this process somewhat well. We did divide the project into smaller tasks and we usually had a good idea of who was working on frontend, backend, or database. The sprints helped us since it felt accomplishing when we completed smaller goals rather than doing a huge task at once. It also was good from transitioning from pure words and ideas into actual code.

What we did not do well is that sometimes we talked in person or over messages and then worked from there instead of fully writing everything in GitHub. We also sometimes had to change who was working on what because of the competencies. Since everyone needed different kinds of evidence for their portfolio, we sometimes had to switch tasks around. So I would say this processes helped us, but we kind of tweaked them in our way to make them more flexible.

What, if anything, got in the way of integrating these process into your workflow?

One thing that got in the way was that the project changed a lot as we worked on it. Since in the first few weeks, we were still figuring out what the MVP should look like and how the frontend, backend, and database should connect that set us back a bit. Also because of that, some tasks changed after we started them, especially once we realized what was realistic or not after talking with PM in our 1on1 meetings.

Another thing that got in the way was the competencies we needed for the class. The competencies were useful because they pushed us to try branch and try different stuff as upcoming software engineers, but they also made task distribution a LOT harder. Sometimes the task that made the most sense for one person skill wise was not the task they needed for their portfolio so we had to move stuff around. Time also got in the way. Since we all had other classes and work, it was easier to just quickly fix something individually or talk about it in some chat or in person instead of making a full like documented issue.
