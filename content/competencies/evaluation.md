**Identify relevant artifacts from the human-centered design process that address the following points:**



### What was the design of your user study to evaluate your artifact?
For the evaluation portion of the project, I showed people the prototype of 4 year planner an asked them whether the layout made sense to them, whether the semester tables were clear enough and in a good place, and whether students could tell what information was being shown to them through the website. Of course this was not the final product, so the user study's main point was to see if we are moving in the right direction in our planning.

The people that participated were asked to explain what they thought the use of the website was before no prior information and then after explaining, I asked them if everything was clear and proper. I also asked them for more suggestions on making it more clear to use before we really dwelve into the development of it.

---

### What were the results of this user study?
The main result we took away is that users like the idea of having everything in the same place as we have noticed from all of our human-centered design processes. They liked that the four year schedule and progress is in the same page, as it is annoying to navigate through multiple pages in the alternative way. 

We also learned that people wanted the interface to be simpler. People thought that we should not make the page too crowded with features but focus on the main points of the project. What people seemed to care most is whether a course counts toward major, whether some course needs a prerequisite and so on. So we should focus more on functionality rather than aesthetics.

---

### What were the main points of future development that you took away from this activity?
The most important point for fututre development was that we should prioritize requirement checking. So we prioritize making sure our web-app allows the users understand whether the courses they put satisfy the major requirements and if they were on track to get the major. Then we thought more about the importance of checking prerequisites, and giving clear warnings if a course cannot be taken for some certain reason. So basically we mainly wanted the planner to be better at explaining why a plan works or does not work, and clear and easy to use.
