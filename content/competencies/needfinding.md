###  Identify relevant artifacts from the human-centered design process that address the following points.


### What techniques did you employ to discover users' needs?
For our needfinding, our main technique used were the interviews we made with students, in particular students that had different uses of a 4 year plan (3-2 student, freshman, third year, etc.). The interview in general was about how these students plan their 4 year schedules and what problems they run into. More specifically we asked them questions about what tools they use to make their 4 year schedules, where do they keep track of them, how often they change them and what do they find most problematic or frustrating when doing so. We continued doing interviews since they gave us specific examples of how users make their plans and also some repeated patterns of problems that the students encounter.

---

### What did you learn about users' needs from this process?
From these interviews, we learned that one of the biggest problems students run into when planning schedules is that the courses and the information is not all in one place. Students have to look at major requirements, which courses are offered, prerequisites, schedules, and how many credits they have separately, which makes planning more annoying and more time consuming for them. We also learned that 4 year plans are never 'static' and need to have lots of backups since the students might not get in the classes they expect to get in, the schedules of the courses conflict with each other or some course might not always be offered in a certain semeter.

### One Interview that I conducted
1. What year are you?
 - Second year.
2. What is your major?
 - Physics and Mathematics.
3. How confident were you with your course planning before starting school at Grinnell?
- I was fairly indifferent and did not really care much about what specific courses I took at the beginning.
4. How did you plan your 4-year schedule?
- I looked through the course catalog and the major requirements needed for graduation.
5. Where do you usually write your plan down, if anywhere?
-  I use Google Docs and write my plan out in a simple row format.
6. Without talking to your adviser, what was your idea for your 4-year plan? What did you do?
- I mainly looked through the course catalog and focused on what was required to graduate.
7. What is the most frustrating part of planning your 4-year schedule?
- Not everything is in one place. It is especially frustrating to find out when courses are offered and to realize that some courses are not available during certain semesters.
8. Have you ever realized too late that a course didn’t count for a major requirement, or that something had a prerequisite you did not know?
- No, because I was attentive when checking requirements and prerequisites.
9. How often do you change your 4-year plan?
- It is mostly set, but I revisit it every time course registration opens.
10. What usually causes you to change it?
-  I check the times of day and days of the week for my courses. If I have too many classes back-to-back or if courses overlap, I may change what I take.
11. How easy or hard is it to tell whether you’re on track to graduate?
-  It is pretty difficult, since I constantly have to check whether I am on track and whether I have enough credits.
12. How do you currently check which requirements you’ve completed?
- I check my Google Doc, which contains a list of required courses, and I manually track the number of credits I have completed and still need to complete each semester.
13. How does planning your 4-year schedule make you feel?
- It feels boring.
14. Do you ever avoid planning because it feels overwhelming?
- Yes. There is a lot to go through, including major requirements and general graduation requirements, which makes the process feel like a lot of work.
15. Is there anything about course planning at Grinnell that we didn’t ask but you think is important?
- No, nothing that I can think of.
16. What would make planning your 4-year schedule easier or less stressful?
- A tool that automatically checks credit requirements and major requirements would make the process much easier.
